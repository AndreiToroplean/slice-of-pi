# Slice of Pi — Wild Ideas

> Ideas to pick from someday, less thought through than the rest of the design. Nothing here is decided.

## Modes

- **Digitless mode.** The digits don't show anywhere: no labels on the keys, no typed digits, only colors, constellations, seasons and sounds. The player learns π without ever seeing it, like a game of repeating sequences of lights and sounds (`meaning.md` §3).
- **Start anywhere.** The player picks where in π to start, for example 100 digits from the millionth place on, and learns that slice (`meaning.md` §2).

## A story

The game still lacks a story to anchor its look. Some threads to pull on:

- **Toys.** The toys of the 90s and 2000s: electronic memory toys, and tabletop games played with other people.
- **The name is a pun.** Sweets and cake don't have to be part of the story, and the icon could change.
- **A memorization technique.** The main menu could reflect one, such as the memory palace below.

## Layers to unlock

The game starts in black and white, and the player unlocks memory layers as they progress: colors first, then the others. Unlocking gives new players a reason to keep playing. A layer stays unlocked for good: restarting from the beginning of π doesn't hide it again.

## Fading stars

Constellations glow for a while after the player types their group, then fade, maybe exponentially, until playing that group again lights them up. The sky then shows what the player knows well and what is slipping away.

## The memory palace

A layer where π becomes a walk through a place, like the memory palaces memory champions use.

- **A world from the seed.** The seed and π generate a simple 2D map. The player's character starts in their room and moves through the map as they type: a door with a keypad opens with the next digits, and so on. It's mostly random, but it must be pretty, so that it's worth the walk.
- **Like a board game.** The character moves from cell to cell along a single path, like a pawn: cheap to draw and animate, and players accept one path on a board where an open 2D world would feel restrictive.
- **Turns.** Now and then the path turns left or right, and the digits decide which way, for example an even digit turns right. Remembering the turn recalls the digit.
- **Encounters.** The character meets challenges along the way, such as a bear to fight by typing the next digits, each one taking from its health bar. The same bear always stands at the same place for a given seed, so a sequence of digits becomes the key to beating it. Making it a race against time is tempting, but it would push the player's timing around.
- **Seasons in the world.** On a real map, the seasons could be seasons of the world rather than colors of the screen.

The open question is the layout. A view from above shows the ground, not the sky. Typing the digits on a single row, scrolling sideways, would free room for the character and the map, and a view from the side would keep the sky above them.
