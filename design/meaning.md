# Slice of Pi — Meaning

> Status: draft · What the game is about and why it's called that, from Andrei (2026-10-10). The rest of the design builds on it: [`vision.md`](vision.md) says how.

## 1. The game is about you

Slice of Pi looks like a game about π, but it's really a game about **your own memory**. π is the material; what the player discovers is themselves: how much they can hold, what their memory does with it, and **what sticks best** for them. Is it the colors? The seasons? The constellations? The sounds we'll add later? The digits themselves?

That question never closes. There will always be a new layer to try (`vision.md` §5), so the game never stops either.

Everything in it belongs to the player: **their** sky, **their** constellations, **their** groupings, **their** slice of π.

## 2. Why "Slice of Pi"

Andrei picked the name 10 to 15 years ago, long before the rest of the design existed, and it has grown several meanings since:

- **You'll only ever know a slice.** No one can learn all of π. However good the player gets, what they know is one slice of it.
- **Your slice.** Players don't even have to start at the beginning: someone might decide to learn 100 digits from the millionth place on, just because (`vision.md` §8).
- **A slice of life.** Playing means giving the game a little time from your life. In return, it should bring you joy.

The name's first picture was a π cut out of cake: sponge with two layers of cream in between and a strawberry glaze on top, next to a cake server. It's now the game's icon ([`icon/cake-pi.png`](icon/cake-pi.png)).

## 3. A memory game first

At heart, it's a cousin of the classic electronic memory game for kids: colored buttons light up and play sounds in a sequence, the player repeats it, and each round the sequence gets one step longer. Slice of Pi works the same way, with π as a sequence that never ends.

Which means π's digits are just one of the cues. The game would still work without them showing at all (`vision.md` §8).
